Privacy Policy

Last updated October 16, 2026

SnapSplit splits a video you choose into clips on your iPhone. The app does not require an account.

Videos and Photos access

When you choose a video through Apple's Photos picker, SnapSplit receives that selected video and saves a copy in the app's private Application Support storage on your iPhone. It uses the copy to play the video, show timeline thumbnails, and export the clips you request. The picker does not give SnapSplit access to your other photos or videos.

SnapSplit automatically saves a project with the source-video copy, your split points, and which clips have been saved. This lets you leave the editor and return to the project later. Projects stay on your device until you delete them in SnapSplit or uninstall the app. Keeping projects can use significant device storage, especially for long videos.

When you save a clip, SnapSplit requests permission to add videos to Photos. It uses that permission only to save the clips you request. Your saved clips remain in Photos and may sync through iCloud Photos if you have that enabled. SnapSplit does not post clips to Snapchat or any other service.

Data sent or shared

SnapSplit does not upload your videos, projects, clips, or app usage to our servers. The app has no ads, tracking, analytics, or third-party SDKs. SnapSplit does not itself transmit video content over the internet. Apple handles the Photos picker, Photos library, iCloud Photos, device backups, and TestFlight under Apple's own privacy practices.

iOS may include SnapSplit's saved projects in an iCloud or computer device backup, depending on your backup settings. That can include the source-video copy and project details. These backups are managed through your device and Apple, not by SnapSplit's servers.

If you use a TestFlight beta, the developer can see the email address used to invite you, your testing status, beta installation and session metrics, and crash reports through Apple. If you send feedback, the developer can also receive your comments, screenshots, and associated tester and device details. We use that information only to manage testing, fix problems, and improve SnapSplit; the app has no separate analytics service.

Retention, deletion, and your choices

Deleting a project in SnapSplit removes its on-device source-video copy and project details, including split points and saved-clip status. Clips already exported to Photos remain there until you delete them in Photos. Uninstalling SnapSplit removes the app's local projects and temporary files from your iPhone. Earlier device backups may still contain project files and are controlled by your backup settings.

After creating a project, SnapSplit removes the picker's temporary import. It removes temporary exported files after saving or an export failure. If the app is interrupted, temporary files may remain. SnapSplit checks for older leftovers when it loads your projects, and iOS may also clear temporary storage.

You choose which video to open. You can revoke SnapSplit's permission to add to Photos in iPhone Settings at any time; this does not delete clips already saved. SnapSplit has no account or server-held videos to delete. You can stop TestFlight testing in TestFlight. We keep tester information and feedback available to us in App Store Connect while the beta is active and delete any copies we download after the related issue is resolved. Apple controls retention of its own TestFlight records. To ask us to remove you from testing or delete beta feedback in our control, contact SnapSplit support.
